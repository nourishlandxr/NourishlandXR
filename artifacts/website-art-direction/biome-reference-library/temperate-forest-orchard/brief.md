# Temperate European forest and orchard — Joey production brief

Status: proposed and queued as the fourth biome-detail pass after the current wheel work is complete.

## Role in the Nourishland system

This fourth biome extends the platform into European and cooler-climate learning. It should connect a natural mixed temperate forest to a traditional fruit orchard and then introduce selected food-forest layers. Students should be able to compare unmanaged woodland structure, managed orchard spacing and regenerative understory design within one continuous landscape.

## Intent

Create a recognizably temperate landscape with seasonal character, open woodland depth and productive fruit trees. The biome should feel distinct from the dense tropical rainforest through trunk visibility, wider spacing, deciduous crown forms, leaf-litter ground and a cooler palette. The orchard should remain culturally familiar and readable while demonstrating how shrubs, herbs, groundcovers and habitat features can enrich it.

## Spatial sequence

Organize the biome as a legible progression:

1. Mixed temperate woodland.
2. Woodland edge and clearing.
3. Traditional fruit orchard.
4. Orchard enriched with food-forest layers.
5. Productive path connecting back toward the wider permaculture system.

The transition should be gradual enough to feel ecological, while each management type remains visually distinguishable for students.

## Temperate forest structure

- Use several deciduous crown families with varied age, spread and trunk form.
- Keep a visible trunk layer and broken sightlines through the woodland.
- Include leaf litter, fallen branches, stones, fungi or decomposing wood in restrained amounts.
- Add shrubs and woodland herbs in clusters rather than a continuous dense carpet.
- Reserve conifers for cooler slopes, higher ground or mixed stands.
- Use small clearings and edge habitat to create light variation and learning opportunities.
- Avoid tropical palms, dense vines and uniformly closed canopy treatment.

## Traditional orchard section

- Establish a recognizable orchard with mature fruit trees at practical, generous spacing.
- Use an irregular or gently ordered layout that follows the terrain rather than a rigid screen-space grid.
- Keep grass, meadow or grazed ground visible beneath much of the orchard.
- Suggested cooler-climate fruit identity: apple, pear, plum, cherry and quince, subject to later species approval.
- Allow differences in crown form, blossom, fruit and seasonal color to communicate orchard diversity.
- Include an accessible path and one gathering or teaching point without dominating the landscape.

## Food-forest completion layer

Demonstrate how a conventional orchard can gain woodland functions:

- canopy: established fruit and selected nut trees;
- lower tree layer: smaller fruit trees;
- shrub layer: berries and currants;
- herb layer: culinary, medicinal and pollinator plants;
- ground layer: low edible or soil-covering plants;
- root layer: selected perennial roots;
- climbers: limited vines on dedicated supports or robust trees;
- habitat layer: logs, stones, flowers and nesting opportunities.

Keep some orchard rows visually simple so students can compare the traditional and layered approaches directly.

## Season and palette

- Design the master composition to work in a green growing-season state first.
- Add restrained autumn variation as the strongest visual distinction from tropical areas.
- Possible later seasonal states: blossom, summer fruit, autumn color and winter structure.
- Use cool greens, moss, grey-brown trunks and muted meadow colors as the base.
- Concentrate amber, ochre and red accents in selected crowns and ground litter.
- Avoid an artificial wall of saturated orange trees.

## Water and terrain relationship

- Continue the project-wide contour-led water logic into this biome.
- Direct clean overflow through vegetated channels, small infiltration basins or orchard swales where suitable.
- Keep orchard roots out of permanently flooded ground unless species selection supports it.
- Use small wet margins or seasonal depressions to connect toward the wetland system.
- Maintain visible terrain slope so woodland type, drainage and management decisions can be taught spatially.

## Movement and educational cues

- Gentle independent leaf and branch movement, lighter than tropical palm motion.
- Occasional falling leaves in the autumn presentation, kept sparse.
- One restrained cooler-climate wildlife cue such as a deer glimpse, woodland bird, hedgehog-scale movement or pollinator activity.
- Future learning anchors should compare woodland layers, orchard spacing, succession, pollination, seasonal cycles and soil cover.

## Website hero treatment

- Use mixed crown colors, visible trunks and one orchard opening to make the cooler biome readable during the outward glimpse.
- Show only enough orchard geometry to imply managed production beyond the central natural composition.
- Use a curving path to lead the eye from woodland into orchard or food forest.
- Keep seasonal color as an accent so it does not compete with the central water and rainforest composition.
- Preserve the master poster/video camera and first-frame matching contract.

## Unity XR treatment

- Separate deciduous families, conifers, orchard trees, food-forest layers, paths, habitat elements and seasonal variants into named groups.
- Prepare tree families for instancing and multiple LOD levels.
- Keep blossom, fruit and autumn foliage as swappable seasonal states where practical.
- Use simplified collision for main trunks, rocks, teaching features and visitor routes.
- Provide anchors for orchard-tree identity, woodland layers, pollination, decomposition and seasonal comparison.
- Allow traditional orchard and enriched food forest to be toggled or compared without rebuilding terrain.

## Avoid

- Treating the entire area as orange autumn forest.
- A dense tropical understory that hides temperate trunks and paths.
- Making conifers the dominant identity unless the biome is later redefined as boreal or alpine.
- A rigid commercial monoculture orchard.
- Filling every orchard gap with food-forest plants and losing the teaching comparison.
- Dramatic mountains copied from references when unsupported by the wheel terrain.
- Copying source imagery, exact arrangements or using photographs as public textures.

## Acceptance criteria

- The biome reads as temperate and cooler-climate from the hero camera without a label.
- Natural woodland, traditional orchard and layered food forest are visually distinguishable.
- Deciduous crown, trunk and ground-layer variation prevents repetitive placement.
- Conifers support an upland/cooler sub-area without taking over the biome.
- Orchard spacing remains readable and believable.
- Food-forest layers enrich selected orchard areas while preserving comparison zones.
- Seasonal color is controlled and compositionally useful.
- Unity assets support seasonal variants, instancing, LODs and educational anchors.

## Decisions to confirm during detailed design

- Exact European climatic region represented: Atlantic, continental, Mediterranean upland or generalized temperate.
- Approved orchard species and whether nuts are included.
- Default season for the website hero.
- Degree of managed order in the traditional orchard.
- Whether students can switch seasonal states in Unity XR.
- Connection point to the wider permaculture and water system.
