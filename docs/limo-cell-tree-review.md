# LIMO cell tree and review

Current local structure · 9 October 2026. Source: `app/services/limoProjectLearning.js` and `app/services/limoSpatialExperience.js`.

## Purpose

Connect spatial plant and Note discoveries into an understanding of a place: observe, understand relationships, imagine a next step, act, and return to learn from change.

## Current tree

Six roots, each with five topics. There are no further botanical or conceptual child cells beneath those thirty topics.

### Read this place

Begin with what this land shows you. Keep measured conditions, recorded observations and unanswered questions distinct.

- **Sun & shade**
  - Question: What is the exposure at this place at this time?
  - Next step: Repeat at another time of day and record the date. A single visit is not a whole-day sun survey.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Water & drainage**
  - Question: What happened to water here during your visit?
  - Next step: Return after rain. Record where water gathers and moves before planning earthworks.
  - Record lens: notes
- **Soil & ground cover**
  - Question: How much of this small patch is covered?
  - Next step: Compare the same patch later; record cover and soil observations separately.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Seasons & extremes**
  - Question: Which condition did you notice here?
  - Next step: Compare dated site notes with a nearby weather station. Climate guidance cannot describe this exact patch by itself.
  - Record lens: notes
- **Access & constraints**
  - Question: What most needs attention before work here?
  - Next step: Mark the constraint and ask the steward before changing access, habitat or infrastructure.
  - Record lens: notes

### Meet the life here

Find the plants and recorded visitors in this Area. Missing records mean we have more to notice; they do not mean life is absent.

- **Plants & forest layers**
  - Question: What role does this recorded plant occupy here?
  - Next step: Open its PIMO, then record the local height and neighbours. A profile layer is authored guidance until checked here.
  - Record lens: layers
- **Wildlife visitors**
  - Question: What did you observe during this visit?
  - Next step: Record the behaviour and time. Keep identity uncertain unless it can be supported.
  - Record lens: notes
- **Damage & beneficial life**
  - Question: What can you see on the selected plant?
  - Next step: Photograph the pattern and monitor change. Identify the cause before choosing a treatment.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Flowers, fruit & change**
  - Question: What stage did you see on this plant today?
  - Next step: Revisit the same plant, count or photograph the stage and compare dates.
  - Record lens: plants
- **What is unrecorded?**
  - Question: What would make this Area easier to understand?
  - Next step: Choose one gap and gather a dated observation. Unassessed is different from absent.
  - Record lens: unknown

### See how it works

Investigate a relationship between real neighbours. A possible benefit is a question until local evidence supports it.

- **Guilds & neighbours**
  - Question: Which interaction should we investigate here?
  - Next step: Inspect the actual neighbours and keep the relationship proposed until supported locally.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Shade, shelter & support**
  - Question: What support is visible beside this plant?
  - Next step: Record who provides the support, who receives it and when it matters.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Water, mulch & soil cycles**
  - Question: Which process can you observe in this patch?
  - Next step: Record the source, amount or coverage and return to check what changed.
  - Record lens: notes
- **Pollination & habitat**
  - Question: What did the visitor do during your observation?
  - Next step: Record behaviour without assuming pollination or a pest-control benefit.
  - Record lens: notes
- **Techniques in this Project**
  - Question: Which example can you investigate here?
  - Next step: Open the linked plant or Area records, then record the intended function and local evidence.
  - Record lens: notes

### Imagine what comes next

Build a small, editable vision around people, existing life and available care. Compare possibilities before changing the land.

- **People, purpose & care**
  - Question: What should this small plan prioritise?
  - Next step: Compare two options against that purpose and the time people can actually give.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Bare land or established?**
  - Question: What starting condition did you observe?
  - Next step: Inventory existing life and constraints first; an open view is not proof that a place is empty.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Which plants could fit?**
  - Question: What would you check before proposing a plant?
  - Next step: Inspect PIMO guidance and site evidence. Keep every candidate proposed until its suitability is checked.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Compare two plans**
  - Question: Which purpose should both alternatives answer?
  - Next step: Draft a small pilot and an observation-first alternative. Neither changes the existing inventory.
  - Record lens: plants
- **First steps & later roles**
  - Question: What scale of change is manageable now?
  - Next step: Define a first phase, a review date and the condition that would justify expanding it.
  - Record lens: plants

### Try something useful

Choose one manageable action, explain why it belongs here, and plan how you will check its result.

- **A small first planting**
  - Question: What needs checking before this pilot?
  - Next step: Draft one small pilot with a care commitment and review date. Confirm it with the steward before planting.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Care for this Area**
  - Question: What care question will you investigate?
  - Next step: Record the issue and a proposed response. Do not infer a treatment from a missing record.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Try a technique**
  - Question: Which small trial could answer your question?
  - Next step: Keep a comparison patch where appropriate. Record the intention before acting, then mark Tried only after you do it.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Harvest, prepare & share**
  - Question: What would you check before using this harvest?
  - Next step: Read the actual PIMO uses and safety guidance. Identification, preparation and permission still need confirmation.
  - Record lens: plants
- **Record a question**
  - Question: What kind of evidence will you gather?
  - Next step: Keep the question tied to this Area or plant and choose a return date.
  - Record lens: all

### See what changed

Return to the same place and question. A new observation can change the plan; opening a cell alone is not progress in the field.

- **Repeat an observation**
  - Question: What changed since the earlier visit?
  - Next step: Compare the same target and method. Keep earlier observations alongside this one.
  - Record lens: journal
- **Compare Areas & dates**
  - Question: What might explain the difference?
  - Next step: Choose comparable observations. Different dates, methods and weather can change what you see.
  - Record lens: journal
- **Results & care effort**
  - Question: What has the activity shown so far?
  - Next step: Review the recorded intention and effort before repeating or expanding the activity.
  - Record lens: journal
- **Investigate a surprise**
  - Question: What should happen next?
  - Next step: Keep the unexpected result; do not overwrite it with the intended outcome.
  - Record lens: journal
- **Choose the next step**
  - Question: What does the evidence support now?
  - Next step: Save the decision and its reason, then select a new review date if needed.
  - Record lens: journal

## What happens after a topic opens

1. Read its question and suggested investigation.
2. Find matching Project records, choose an Area, and inspect a PIMO or Note.
3. Record an observation, draft a trial, or compare plans.
4. Choose one of three prepared responses or mark it uncertain.
5. Set a return date and save a personal notebook record.
6. Revisit that record and add a later observation. Saving a Project Note is a separate explicit action where supported.

## Review findings

- **The visible depth is limited.** The authored spatial tree ends at each topic; deeper learning becomes a sequence of forms and actions rather than another branch of learning cells.
- **The topics are useful questions, but the teaching depth is thin.** Each topic has one question, three prepared answers and one next step. It has no dedicated explanation, illustrated example or deeper conceptual subtopic.
- **Project statistics were dominating the summary.** This repair makes the learning purpose or question the leading summary; counts remain available as supporting evidence.
- **The Project record lens is broad.** Many different topics return the same plant inventory. Neighbour relationships, water movement, observed wildlife and care outcomes are not automatically established by those plant records.
- **Vision is a template rather than a developed design lesson.** Its two generated plans are a small pilot and an observation-first alternative; they are not yet detailed, place-specific proposals.
- **The six-root tree differs from the older illustrated Eye / Learning / Vision entry language.** Those pictures can introduce the learning, but their names do not currently mirror every root in this tree.

## Recommended discussion

Keep the spatial storytelling foundation. Decide whether Eye / Learning / Vision should become the primary entry points, with these six roots underneath, or whether the six-root cycle should remain visible. Then add depth beneath selected topics: an explanation, a local example, a connection to actual plants and neighbours, an observation, and a possible action. This should precede adding more statistics or notebook controls.
